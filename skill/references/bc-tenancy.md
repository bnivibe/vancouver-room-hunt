# BC tenancy law for a room renter

Checked against the Residential Tenancy Act text at www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca and
the government's own pages at www2.gov.bc.ca in 2026-09. This is a working
summary for reading listings and agreements, not legal advice: read the
section itself before relying on it, and use the Residential Tenancy Branch
(RTB) or the Civil Resolution Tribunal (CRT) for a real dispute.

## First question: is it a tenancy at all?

| Arrangement | Status | Protection |
|---|---|---|
| Renter shares a kitchen or bathroom **with the owner** | Excluded from the Act, s.4(c) | Contract and common law only |
| Renter is the roommate of a **tenant** who holds the lease | "Occupant", not a tenant | None under the Act; the CRT handles roommate money disputes |
| Owner, or an operator, signs and **does not live in the unit** | Tenancy | The Act applies, even with a shared kitchen among tenants |

- **s.1 "landlord"** includes (a) the owner's agent and (c) "a person, other
  than a tenant occupying the rental unit, who is entitled to possession of the
  rental unit and exercises any of the rights of a landlord". So an operator who
  places roommates and collects rent but lives elsewhere is a landlord whether
  they own the house or head-lease it.
- **s.5**: the Act cannot be contracted out of. Calling the paper "a simple
  contract" does not remove it.
- **s.16**: rights and obligations start when the agreement is made, not at
  move-in.

Ask "does the person I sign with live here after I move in?" before judging any
agreement, and write the answer into the record.

**Owner or head tenant still matters for stability.** When a middle person
collects the rent, find out which kind: the owner's agent, or a head tenant
re-letting rooms. Under the Act either is the landlord, so the renter keeps
protection. The risk sits in the head-tenant case without the owner's consent:
**s.34(1)** forbids subletting without the landlord's written consent, and
**s.47(1)(i)** lets the owner end that tenancy for cause on at least a month's
notice, taking the sub-tenants with it. Evidence that lowers the risk: a building
made for room rental (BC Assessment bedroom and bathroom counts), residents who
have paid the same person for a year or more, an operator with a public identity.
An LTSA title names the owner, not the middle person's authority.

## Deposits

- **s.19(1)**: a security deposit cannot exceed half of one month's rent. A
  deposit of exactly half a month suggests a landlord who knows the law; more than
  that is recoverable and a warning about the rest of the agreement. Recompute the
  cap whenever the rent changes.
- **s.20(e)**: a term letting the landlord keep the deposit automatically is
  prohibited.
- **s.38(1) and (4)**: the deposit comes back within 15 days of the later of the
  tenancy ending and the forwarding address arriving; deductions need the tenant's
  written consent or an RTB order.
- **Move-in condition inspection**: a landlord who skips it (form RTB-27) loses
  the right to claim against the deposit for damage. Take dated photos anyway.
- For an occupant (no tenancy), half a month is a convention to hold to, not a
  rule to cite.

## Living there

- **s.29**: the landlord enters only with consent at the time or 24 hours'
  written notice. It does not bind roommates.
- **s.31(3)**: a tenant may not change the lock on the rental unit without the
  landlord's written consent. If the bedroom has no lock, ask in writing to fit a
  keyed knob at your own cost, hand one key over, and restore the original on
  move-out. If refused: a lockable box cabled to heavy furniture.
- Fees are limited by regulation: a late fee is at most CAD 25, a key costs its
  replacement cost. "Fines" are not a permitted fee, and pre-set damages must be a
  genuine pre-estimate, not a penalty.
- The privacy commissioner's guidance is that landlords should not keep copies
  of ID documents. Show ID; do not hand over a passport copy.
- A strata unit is bound by the strata's bylaws; ask for them (and the Form K the
  strata uses for tenants).

## Ending it

- **s.45(1)**: a tenant ends a periodic (month-to-month) tenancy with one clear
  month's notice.
- **s.45(2)**: a tenant cannot end a fixed term early. Leaving means owing rent
  until the unit is re-let (the landlord must mitigate).
- **s.34(2) and (3)**: while six months or more remain on a fixed term, the
  landlord must not unreasonably withhold consent to assign or sublet, and must
  not charge for considering it. So the first half of a 12-month term has a legal
  exit and the second half does not. Say this when pricing any 1-year offer.
- **s.44(3)**: at the end of a fixed term the tenancy becomes month-to-month on
  the same terms, unless the agreement carries a vacate clause the regulation
  permits. A 1-year term is not an eviction date.
- **A loose notice term is fixed by asymmetry, not refusal.** "Just tell me a day
  before" cuts both ways; write it as 14 days from the renter and 30 from the
  host. Hosts usually accept, because what they need is warning that a room is
  emptying.

## Reading the agreement

Read the blanks, not the boilerplate: rent, the fixed-term end date, the
services-included checklist (make "no extra charges" written), the deposit, any
vacate clause, whose name is the landlord, and the landlord's address for service.
Korean-board room templates (party A and party B, "deposit deducted in full",
fines, passport copies, fixed check-in and check-out hours) are common and partly
unenforceable where the Act applies. Being "registered as an occupant" removes the
risk of being found as an unauthorised occupant; it does not survive the head
tenancy ending.

## Money order

- **Deposit at signing, first month's rent on key day.** An occupant who pays
  and is not let in has no RTB remedy, so this order matters most there. Under a
  real tenancy, paying the first month at signing is ordinary and recoverable.
- **Fill in the blanks together, then transfer, then both sign.** The landlord is
  still paid before signing, and the terms are on paper before the money leaves.
- **Interac e-Transfer with Autodeposit shows the recipient's registered legal
  name** before you confirm. That, plus the LTSA title, settles who is paid. It
  says nothing about the terms.

## The renter's tax credit

The B.C. renter's tax credit is real and refundable (up to CAD 400 a year at the
time of checking), but there is **no registration**: the renter claims it on form
BC479 with the T1 return, giving the address, total rent, months of tenancy and
the landlord's name. It needs **at least six one-month periods** in the calendar
year, so an October move-in earns nothing for that year, and it phases out over a
band of family income (roughly CAD 66,000 to 86,000 when checked; confirm the
current year). Never weigh a listing on it. The valuable half is the paperwork: a
written agreement in the renter's name is proof of address for a licence, a bank
and a credit file.
